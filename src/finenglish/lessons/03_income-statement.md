## Theory

## The waterfall of profit

The income statement (отчёт о прибылях и убытках) is a waterfall: it starts with everything the company earned and subtracts costs in a fixed order until only net income (чистая прибыль) is left. Here is the standard structure.

| Line | What it is | Russian |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (sales, top line) | Money earned from selling goods and services | Выручка |
| − Cost of goods sold, COGS | Direct costs of what was sold: materials, factory labour | Себестоимость |
| = Gross profit | What is left after direct costs | Валовая прибыль |
| − Operating expenses, OPEX (SG&A, R&D) | Salaries of office staff, marketing, rent, research | Операционные расходы |
| = EBITDA | Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization | Прибыль до процентов, налогов и амортизации |
| − Depreciation and amortization, D&A | Wear of equipment and intangible assets spread over years | Амортизация |
| = EBIT (operating profit) | Earnings before interest and taxes | Операционная прибыль |
| − Interest expense (+ interest income) | Cost of debt | Процентные расходы |
| = EBT (pre-tax profit) | Earnings before taxes | Прибыль до налогообложения |
| − Income tax | Corporate tax | Налог на прибыль |
| = Net income (bottom line) | Profit for shareholders | Чистая прибыль |

Two slang words: the top line (верхняя строка) means revenue, the bottom line (нижняя строка, итог) means net income. "Top-line growth" is revenue growth; "it hurts the bottom line" means it reduces profit.

## Revenue: when is a sale a sale?

Revenue is recognised (признаётся) when the company delivers the product or service, not when it receives cash. This is the accrual principle (принцип начисления). If a software company sells a one-year subscription (подписка) for 12,000 roubles in January, it recognises 1,000 roubles of revenue each month. The cash arrived in January, but the revenue is spread over the year. The unearned part sits on the balance sheet as deferred revenue (отложенная выручка), a liability.

This is why revenue can differ from cash collected, and why analysts check receivables (дебиторская задолженность): if revenue grows but receivables grow faster, the company may be booking sales that customers will never pay for.

## COGS and gross margin

Cost of goods sold (себестоимость) includes only the direct costs of the products sold: raw materials, factory workers, energy for the plant. Gross profit divided by revenue is the gross margin (валовая маржа, валовая рентабельность). It shows the basic economics of the product. A software company may have a gross margin of 80%; a supermarket chain, 25%; an oil company, somewhere between, depending on oil prices.

## OPEX, SG&A and R&D

Operating expenses (операционные расходы) are the costs of running the business that are not tied to a specific unit of product. The two big groups are selling, general and administrative expenses, SG&A (коммерческие, общие и административные расходы), which include marketing, head-office salaries and rent, and research and development, R&D (расходы на исследования и разработки).

## EBITDA: the favourite number of bankers

EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortization — прибыль до вычета процентов, налогов и амортизации) is not a line in the official statement under IFRS, but everyone calculates it. Why? Because it shows operating performance (операционные результаты) without three things that depend on history or policy, not on the business: how the company is financed (interest), where it pays tax (taxes) and how old its equipment is (depreciation).

EBITDA margin (рентабельность по EBITDA) = EBITDA / Revenue. It is the most common way to compare the profitability (прибыльность, рентабельность) of companies in one industry. It is also the base for the most popular multiple, EV/EBITDA (Topic 9), and for the main debt ratio, Net debt/EBITDA (Topic 18).

Critics say EBITDA is "earnings before everything bad". It ignores capex (капитальные затраты): a company with huge EBITDA that must spend it all on new equipment is not rich. That is why we also look at cash flow (Topic 5).

## Depreciation and amortization

When a company buys a machine for 100 million roubles that will work for ten years, it does not record a 100 million expense in year one. It records 10 million each year as depreciation (амортизация основных средств). The same logic for intangible assets (нематериальные активы) such as licences and software is called amortization (амортизация нематериальных активов). Both are non-cash expenses (неденежные расходы): the cash left when the machine was bought. This is the main reason profit and cash differ.

## Interest and taxes

Interest expense (процентные расходы) is the cost of debt. It is deducted before tax, so debt reduces the tax bill: the tax shield (налоговый щит). Income tax (налог на прибыль) in Russia is 25% from 2025; in many countries, 20–30%. The effective tax rate (эффективная ставка налога) is tax actually paid divided by pre-tax profit, and it often differs from the official rate.

## Net income and EPS

Net income belongs to shareholders. Divided by the number of shares outstanding (акции в обращении), it gives earnings per share, EPS (прибыль на акцию), the number quoted in every earnings release (пресс-релиз о результатах). Diluted EPS (разводнённая прибыль на акцию) also counts shares that may appear from options and convertible bonds. The company can pay net income out as dividends (дивиденды) or keep it as retained earnings (нераспределённая прибыль).

## Margins: the language of profitability

Every profit line divided by revenue gives a margin (маржа, рентабельность):

- Gross margin = Gross profit / Revenue
- EBITDA margin = EBITDA / Revenue
- Operating margin (операционная рентабельность) = EBIT / Revenue
- Net margin (чистая рентабельность) = Net income / Revenue

When an analyst says "margins are expanding" (маржа растёт), it means profit is growing faster than revenue. "Margin compression" (сжатие маржи) is the opposite, often caused by rising costs the company cannot pass on to customers.

## One-offs and adjusted numbers

Companies often report adjusted EBITDA (скорректированная EBITDA) or adjusted net income, excluding one-off items (разовые статьи): a fine, a write-off (списание), a gain on selling a building. The idea is to show recurring (регулярные, повторяющиеся) results. Be careful: some companies "adjust" every year, and the one-offs become suspiciously regular.

> **Remember.** Revenue → Gross profit → EBITDA → EBIT → EBT → Net income. Each step subtracts one group of costs. EBITDA is the banker's favourite because it ignores financing, tax and the age of equipment.
