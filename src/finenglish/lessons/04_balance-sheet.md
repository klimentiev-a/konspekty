## Theory

## A snapshot, not a film

The balance sheet (бухгалтерский баланс) shows what a company owns and owes on one date, usually 31 December or the last day of a quarter. The two sides must always be equal:

> **Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' equity.** Активы = Обязательства + Собственный капитал.

Think of buying a flat for 10 million roubles with a 7 million mortgage (ипотека) and 3 million of your own money. The flat is the asset (10). The mortgage is the liability (7). Your equity is 3. If the flat's value rises to 12, equity becomes 5; the bank still gets 7.

## Assets: what the company owns

Assets (активы) are listed in order of liquidity (ликвидность), that is, how fast they turn into cash.

**Current assets (оборотные активы)** are expected to become cash within a year:

- Cash and cash equivalents (денежные средства и эквиваленты): money in the bank and short deposits.
- Accounts receivable (дебиторская задолженность): money customers owe for goods already delivered.
- Inventory (запасы): raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.
- Prepaid expenses (расходы будущих периодов): rent or insurance paid in advance.

**Non-current assets (внеоборотные активы)** serve the business for many years:

- Property, plant and equipment, PP&E (основные средства): land, buildings, machines, recorded at cost minus accumulated depreciation (накопленная амортизация).
- Intangible assets (нематериальные активы): licences, software, patents, brands bought from others.
- Goodwill (гудвил): the premium paid above book value when buying another company (Topic 16).
- Long-term investments (долгосрочные финансовые вложения): stakes in other companies.

## Liabilities: what the company owes

Liabilities (обязательства) are also split by time.

**Current liabilities (краткосрочные обязательства)** are due within a year: accounts payable (кредиторская задолженность) to suppliers, short-term debt (краткосрочный долг) and the current portion of long-term debt, accrued expenses (начисленные расходы) such as wages not yet paid, and taxes payable (задолженность по налогам).

**Non-current liabilities (долгосрочные обязательства)**: long-term debt (долгосрочный долг), bonds (облигации), lease liabilities (обязательства по аренде) and deferred tax liabilities (отложенные налоговые обязательства).

## Equity: what belongs to the owners

Shareholders' equity (собственный капитал) has three main parts: share capital (уставный капитал) and share premium (эмиссионный доход) paid by investors when shares were issued, retained earnings (нераспределённая прибыль) accumulated over the years, and treasury shares (казначейские акции), the company's own shares bought back, shown as a negative number. Equity is also called book value (балансовая стоимость). Note that market capitalization (рыночная капитализация), the price of all shares on the exchange, is usually very different from book value, because the market values future profits, while the balance sheet records history.

## Working capital: the money stuck in the business

Net working capital, NWC (чистый оборотный капитал) is the money a company needs to keep day-to-day operations running. The operating definition is:

> **NWC = Receivables + Inventory − Payables.** (A broader definition: current assets minus current liabilities, excluding cash and debt.)

Why does it matter? Because growth eats cash. A retailer that opens new stores must fill them with inventory before it sells anything. A manufacturer that wins a big client must deliver goods and wait 60 days for payment. In both cases profit may look fine, but cash disappears into working capital. An increase in NWC is a cash outflow (отток денег); a decrease is an inflow (приток).

Analysts measure working capital in days:

- Days sales outstanding, DSO (оборачиваемость дебиторской задолженности в днях) = Receivables / Revenue × 365. How long customers take to pay.
- Days inventory outstanding, DIO (оборачиваемость запасов в днях) = Inventory / COGS × 365. How long goods sit in the warehouse.
- Days payable outstanding, DPO (оборачиваемость кредиторской задолженности в днях) = Payables / COGS × 365. How long the company takes to pay suppliers.

The cash conversion cycle (цикл оборота денежных средств) = DSO + DIO − DPO. The shorter, the better. Some businesses have a negative cycle: a supermarket sells goods for cash today and pays suppliers in 45 days, so suppliers finance the store.

## Net debt: the number bankers look at first

Gross debt (валовой долг) is all interest-bearing liabilities (процентные обязательства): loans, bonds, leases. Net debt (чистый долг) subtracts cash:

> **Net debt = Total debt − Cash and equivalents.**

Net debt is used because a company with 100 of debt and 100 of cash is not really in debt. The key ratio is Net debt / EBITDA (чистый долг к EBITDA): below 1x is conservative, 2–3x is normal for a stable business, above 4x is risky. Lenders put this ratio into loan agreements as a covenant (ковенант, Topic 18).

If cash exceeds debt, the company has a net cash position (чистая денежная позиция). In Russia, Surgutneftegas is the famous example, with its huge foreign-currency deposits.

## Leases and off-balance-sheet items

Since IFRS 16 (2019), leases (аренда) are on the balance sheet: a right-of-use asset (актив в форме права пользования) and a lease liability. Before that, long-term rent was off-balance-sheet (забалансовое обязательство), which made retailers and airlines look less indebted than they were. When comparing companies, check whether lease liabilities are included in debt.

## Reading a balance sheet quickly

1. Is there enough liquidity? Compare current assets with current liabilities: the current ratio (коэффициент текущей ликвидности). Below 1 means the company may struggle to pay bills this year.
2. How much debt? Net debt / EBITDA and Debt / Equity (долг к капиталу).
3. Is working capital growing faster than revenue? If yes, something is wrong with collection or inventory.
4. How big is goodwill? Large goodwill means the company grew by acquisitions and may face an impairment (обесценение) if those deals disappoint.

> **Remember.** The balance sheet balances. Net debt = debt − cash. Working capital is cash stuck in receivables and inventory. Growth needs working capital, so growth needs cash.
