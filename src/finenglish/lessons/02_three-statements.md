## Theory

## Why three statements, not one

A company reports its results in financial statements (финансовая отчётность). There are three main ones, and each answers a different question.

| Statement | Question it answers | Period or moment |
|---|---|---|
| Income statement (отчёт о прибылях и убытках, ОФР) | Did the company earn a profit this period? | A period: quarter or year |
| Balance sheet (бухгалтерский баланс) | What does the company own and owe right now? | A moment: the last day of the period |
| Cash flow statement (отчёт о движении денежных средств, ОДДС) | Where did cash come from and where did it go? | A period |

Why not one statement? Because profit (прибыль) and cash (денежные средства) are different things. A company can show a profit and still run out of cash, for example when customers pay late. And the balance sheet is a photo, while the other two are a film of the period.

Together they are often called "the three statements", and the classic interview question is "Walk me through the three statements": explain how a change in one flows into the others.

## The income statement in one paragraph

The income statement (also called the P&L, profit and loss statement) starts with revenue (выручка), the money earned from selling goods and services. Then it subtracts costs step by step: cost of goods sold, COGS (себестоимость), operating expenses (операционные расходы), depreciation (амортизация основных средств) and amortization (амортизация нематериальных активов), interest (проценты) and taxes (налоги). What is left is net income (чистая прибыль). Along the way we get important intermediate lines such as gross profit (валовая прибыль) and operating profit, also called EBIT (Earnings Before Interest and Taxes, прибыль до процентов и налогов). Topic 3 covers this in detail.

## The balance sheet in one paragraph

The balance sheet lists assets (активы) on one side and, on the other, liabilities (обязательства) and shareholders' equity (собственный капитал). The key equation is:

> **Assets = Liabilities + Equity.** Everything the company owns was paid for either with someone else's money (liabilities) or with the owners' money (equity).

Assets are split into current assets (оборотные активы), which turn into cash within a year, such as cash itself, receivables (дебиторская задолженность) and inventory (запасы), and non-current assets (внеоборотные активы), such as property, plant and equipment, PP&E (основные средства) and goodwill (гудвил, деловая репутация). Liabilities are split the same way: current liabilities (краткосрочные обязательства), for example payables (кредиторская задолженность), and long-term debt (долгосрочный долг). Topic 4 goes deeper.

## The cash flow statement in one paragraph

The cash flow statement has three sections. Cash flow from operations, CFO (денежный поток от операционной деятельности) shows cash generated by the core business. Cash flow from investing, CFI (от инвестиционной деятельности) shows money spent on capital expenditures, capex (капитальные затраты) and acquisitions, or received from selling assets. Cash flow from financing, CFF (от финансовой деятельности) shows debt raised or repaid, dividends (дивиденды) paid and shares issued or bought back. The sum of the three is the change in cash for the period.

## How the statements connect

This is the part everyone must know. There are three links.

**Link 1: net income goes to equity.** Net income from the income statement increases retained earnings (нераспределённая прибыль), which is part of equity on the balance sheet. Dividends reduce it.

**Link 2: net income starts the cash flow statement.** CFO begins with net income, then adds back non-cash expenses (неденежные расходы) such as depreciation, and adjusts for changes in working capital (оборотный капитал): if receivables grew, cash is lower than profit, because customers have not paid yet.

**Link 3: the change in cash lands on the balance sheet.** The closing cash on the cash flow statement is the cash line on the balance sheet. Capex increases PP&E; new debt increases liabilities; dividends reduce equity.

## A worked example: depreciation goes up by 10

Interviewers love this question: "Depreciation increases by 10. What happens to the three statements?" Assume a 20% tax rate (ставка налога).

1. **Income statement.** Operating profit falls by 10. Taxes fall by 2. Net income falls by 8.
2. **Cash flow statement.** Start with net income, which is 8 lower. Add back depreciation of 10, because it is a non-cash expense. Cash flow from operations rises by 2. Cash is up by 2.
3. **Balance sheet.** Cash is up 2, PP&E is down 10, so assets are down 8. Retained earnings are down 8, so equity is down 8. Both sides fall by 8: the balance sheet balances (баланс сходится).

The lesson: a higher non-cash expense reduces profit but increases cash, because of the tax saving. This tax effect is called a tax shield (налоговый щит).

## Where the statements come from

Companies prepare statements under an accounting standard (стандарт учёта). International Financial Reporting Standards, IFRS (МСФО, Международные стандарты финансовой отчётности) are used in most of the world, including Russian public companies. US GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles, ОПБУ США) is used in the United States. Russian companies also prepare RAS (Russian Accounting Standards, РСБУ) for tax and legal purposes. The logic is the same; the details differ, for example in how leases (аренда) or inventory are recorded.

Public companies (публичные компании) publish statements every quarter (квартал) and every year. An annual report (годовой отчёт) is audited (проверен аудитором) by an independent auditor (аудитор), which is why the Big Four audit firms (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG) are so important.

> **Remember.** Income statement = performance over a period. Balance sheet = position at a moment. Cash flow statement = movement of cash. Net income links all three.
